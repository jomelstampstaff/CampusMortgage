Mortgage Terminology Session 1

Mortgage Note

Also known as a real estate lien — a promissory note associated with a mortgage loan. It is a written promise to repay a specified sum plus interest at a specified rate and time.

Fixed-Rate Note

Interest rate stays the same for the life of the loan.

Adjustable-Rate Note

Interest rate can change based on an index.

Mortgage vs. Note

DocumentPurposeRecorded?
Mortgage / Deed of TrustPledges title to real property as securityYes — public record
Mortgage NoteWritten promise to repay the debtNo — not recorded

First Mortgage

A mortgage where the lender has first right to full payment before any other lenders or liens on the same property.

Second Mortgage / Subordinate Lien

Placed on a property after another mortgage. Typically used for seller financing, HELOC, or home improvement. Has greater risk, so usually charges a higher interest rate.

HELOC (Home Equity Line of Credit)

Usually a second mortgage structured as a line of credit — borrower can draw up to a maximum at any time. Usually adjustable rate.

🔑 Remember: First mortgage = LTV · Second mortgage = part of CLTV · HELOC = HCLTV (uses full line limit).

Foreclosure Priority Example

Property value (net of sale costs): $100,000
First mortgage balance: $90,000
Second mortgage balance: $115,000
First lender collects: $90,000 + interest + costs
Remaining for second lender: $100,000 − $90,000 = $10,000
Second lender writes off shortage or pursues borrower

Deed of Trust vs. Mortgage

Deed of Trust

Three parties: Trustor (borrower), Beneficiary (lender), Trustee (independent third party holding title).

Mortgage

Two parties: Mortgagor (borrower) and Mortgagee (lender).

Refinance Transactions

Cash-Out Refinance

Borrower takes equity out for debt consolidation or other purpose.

Rate-and-Term / Limited Cash-Out

Restructure financing, reduce rate, or switch from ARM to fixed.

Mortgage Insurance

Protects the lender against loss in case of default. Required on conventional loans with LTV > 80%.

Conventional — PMI

Private Mortgage Insurance. Paid monthly. Borrowers can waive escrow only if LTV ≤ 80%.

FHA — Two Types

Upfront Mortgage Insurance Premium (collected at closing) + monthly insurance premium.

Ratio Terminology Session 1

Loan-to-Value (LTV)

Formula LTV = First Mortgage Loan Amount ÷ Lesser of Appraised Value or Purchase Price

AUS rounds up to the nearest whole percentage when calculating all LTVs.

Loan Amount: $125,000
Appraised Value: $200,000
$125,000 ÷ $200,000 = 62.5%
Round up to nearest whole %
LTV = 63%

Combined Loan-to-Value (CLTV)

Formula CLTV = (First Loan + All Subordinate Liens) ÷ Lesser of Appraised Value or Purchase Price
First Loan: $125,000
Second Lien Balance: $30,000
Total: $125,000 + $30,000 = $155,000
Appraised Value: $200,000
$155,000 ÷ $200,000 = 77.5%
CLTV = 78%

High Combined Loan-to-Value (HCLTV)

Formula HCLTV = (First Loan + Total HELOC Credit Limit) ÷ Lesser of Appraised Value or Purchase Price
⚠️ Critical: For HELOCs, use the full credit line limit — NOT the balance.
First Loan: $125,000
HELOC Line Limit (not balance): $45,000
Total: $125,000 + $45,000 = $170,000
Appraised Value: $200,000
$170,000 ÷ $200,000 = 85%
HCLTV = 85%

PITI & PITIA

PITI

Principal · Interest · Taxes · Insurance

PITIA

PITI + Association dues (HOA). Required when property is in a PUD or has HOA.

Debt-to-Income (DTI)

Front-End / Housing Ratio PITIA ÷ Total Qualifying Income
Back-End / Total DTI (PITIA + All Other Monthly Debts) ÷ Total Qualifying Income
P&I: $500
Property Taxes: $215
Homeowner's Insurance: $95
Mortgage Insurance: $63
HOA Dues: $25
Total PITIA = $500 + $215 + $95 + $63 + $25 = $898
Other Monthly Debts: $1,200
Total Qualifying Income: $4,750
Back-End DTI: ($898 + $1,200) ÷ $4,750 = 44%
Front-End Ratio: $898 ÷ $4,750 = 18.91%

Down Payment, Closing Costs & Cash to Close

TermDefinition
Down PaymentDifference between purchase price/payoff and new loan amount
Closing CostsCosts incurred to purchase or refinance real estate
Cash to CloseCash required to meet down payment + closing costs
Escrow / Impound AccountDeposit account maintained by lender, funded by borrower, used to pay taxes & insurance
Purchase Example:
Sales Price: $400,000 − Loan Amount: $340,000 = Down Payment $60,000
Closing Costs: $12,000
Cash to Close: $60,000 + $12,000 = $72,000
Cash to Close = $72,000
Refinance Example:
Payoff: $276,000 − Loan: $275,000 = Shortfall $1,000
Closing Costs: $3,000
Cash to Close: $1,000 + $3,000 = $4,000
Cash to Close = $4,000
Escrow Waiver: Borrower can waive escrow only if LTV ≤ 80% (20% down). Above 80%, escrow is required.

Government Loan Programs

FHA

Federal Housing Administration (within HUD). Two forms of MI: upfront fee + monthly premium.

VA

Veterans Affairs. No monthly MI, but may have upfront one-time funding fee.

USDA

Rural Development. Uses its own AUS: GUS.

The Loan Cycle Session 1

1Loan Officer — Takes application, consults on programs, locks rate, completes 1003, pulls credit, structures loan.
2Loan Setup — Initial data entry into LOS. Orders flood certs, appraisals, title work.
3Loan Processor — Main point of contact for borrower and LO. Obtains missing docs, packages loan for underwriter.
4Underwriter — Analyzes overall risk, reviews all docs for accuracy, issues final decision.
5Closer — Prepares final closing disclosures and closing package. Wires funds to title company.
🔄 Flow: Loan Officer → Loan Setup → Loan Processor → Underwriter → Closer → Funding

Loan Processor Duties Session 1

Core Responsibilities

  • Review all loan documents for accuracy and completeness
  • Check all pages included, signatures present, legibility, inconsistencies
  • Update loan application and loan notes with status updates
  • Validate expiration dates — docs expire between 90–120 days, 180 days for new construction
  • Order payoff statements and subordination agreements
  • Monitor rate lock expiration dates
  • Pipeline management
  • Keep borrower informed about loan status
  • Receive clear-to-close (CTC) and schedule closing

Recommended File Review Flow

1Review 1008 and 1003
2Check Photo ID — name, DOB, address must match credit report
3Check Social Security card if available
4Review Credit Report — names, DOB, SSN, address, AKAs
5Review Sales Contract — purchase price, all pages, signatures
6Review Income and Assets
7Review Appraisal if in file
8Review Disclosures
💡 Pro tip: A processor certification or memo as a cover page for documentation can significantly reduce the number of conditions you receive from underwriting.

Breaking Down the Forms Session 1

Fannie Mae Form 1003 — Loan Application

Provides detailed information about the loan transaction and borrower's financial history. Requires 2-year address and employment history.

Fannie Mae Form 1008 — Underwriting Transmittal

Provides detailed loan characteristics: LTV, DTI, method of underwriting, agency used.

Regulation B (ECOA)

Prohibits lenders from discriminating against credit applicants due to race, religion, sex, and more.

RESPA-TILA (Loan Estimate)

Must be given to consumer within 3 business days after submission of loan application. Binding — fees have almost no tolerance of moving.

📌 Three disclosures within 3 days of application:
1. TIL / Loan Estimate
2. RESPA-TILA
3. ECOA & Servicing Disclosure

Closing Disclosure (CD) Session 2

In October 2015, the CD replaced the HUD-1 Settlement Agreement. It merged the HUD-1, GFE, and Truth in Lending into one 5-page document.

🚨 Critical Rule: The CD must be delivered to the borrower at least 3 business days before loan closing. No exceptions.

Delivery Timing Example

CD issued Friday, April 15
Day 1: Friday (April 15)
Day 2: Saturday (April 16)
Day 3: Monday (April 18)
Earliest closing date: Tuesday, April 19

CD Page by Page — Key Checkpoints Session 2

Page 1 Checkpoints

  • Date issued, closing date, disbursement date — these should never match
  • Settlement agent info, file number
  • Sales price must match LOS, 1008, 1003
  • Borrower and seller info must match exactly
  • HOA dues shown but NOT included in escrow

Page 2 Checkpoints

  • Origination charges, underwriting fee, application fee
  • Appraisal fee — often paid outside closing (POC)
  • Services borrower did/didn't shop for
  • Other costs: recording, transfer taxes, prepaids, escrow

Page 3 Checkpoints

  • Cash to close comparison between LE and CD
  • Summary of transactions — borrower side and seller side

Page 4 Checkpoints

  • Assumability, demand feature, negative amortization
  • Partial payments accepted?
  • Escrow account — yes/no

Page 5 Checkpoints

  • Loan calculations: total payments, finance charge, APR, TIP
  • Contact information: lender, broker, agents, settlement agent
  • Confirm Receipt: Must be signed and dated

Verifications Session 3

Verification of Deposit (VOD)

Used to verify assets, bank loan terms, and payment history. Must be fully completed — no blank sections.

Verification of Employment (VOE)

Written VOE

Form completed by employer. Must be fully completed — circle needed fields.

Verbal VOE

Done at the end of the loan, within 10 days of closing.

⚠️ Don't forget: Borrower's Authorization must accompany all VOEs, VODs, and VORs.

Verification of Rent (VOR) / Mortgage (VOM)

Used to verify housing payment terms and payment history over the most recent 12 months.

Purchase Contract Session 3

Checklist

  • Estimated close date — if expired, extension required
  • All pages initialed and signed
  • Selling authority — seller signature vs. vested owner on title
  • Purchase price changes on addendums
  • Seller credits for closing costs
  • Borrowers on contract match those on 1003
  • Address matches everywhere
  • Legal description matches title
  • FHA loans: realtor license numbers required
Seller Contribution Limits: FHA max 6% · Conventional 3% (based on LTV/occupancy)
