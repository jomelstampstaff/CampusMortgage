1. What is FHA? Module 3 · Session 1

FHA stands for the Federal Housing Administration — a government agency within the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD), created by Congress in 1934.

An FHA loan is a mortgage insured by the FHA. This insurance protects the lender from loss if the borrower defaults — which allows lenders to offer more flexible qualification guidelines than conventional loans.

🏛️ Agency

Federal Housing Administration (within HUD)

📅 Created

1934 by U.S. Congress

🛡️ Purpose

Insure mortgages so lenders can offer more flexible terms

🔑 Key concept: The FHA does not lend money — it insures loans made by approved lenders. That's why FHA loans are called government-insured rather than government-funded.

2. FHA Loan Benefits Module 3 · Session 1

💰 Low Down Payment

As little as 3.5% down payment

📊 Minimum Credit Score

As low as 500 (with restrictions)

📈 Higher DTI Allowed

Only if approved through FHA Total Scorecard (FHA's AUS)

Two Key Perks of FHA

  • Low 3.5% down payment — much lower than conventional loans
  • More flexible qualification guidelines — helpful for borrowers with lower credit scores or higher DTIs

Why Mortgage Insurance Premiums Matter

FHA mortgage insurance premiums are based on LTV of the loan, not on credit scores. This makes FHA mortgage payments more predictable and often more affordable for many borrowers, since the government-backed insurance encourages lenders to offer these loans to a broader range of creditworthy borrowers.

🔑 Remember: FHA MI is LTV-based, not credit-score-based. That's different from how conventional LLPAs work.

3. UFMIP & Annual MIP Module 3 · Session 1

Every FHA borrower pays two types of mortgage insurance premiums:

1️⃣ Upfront Mortgage Insurance Premium (UFMIP)
  • One-time fee paid on all FHA loans
  • 1.75% of the base loan amount
  • Normally financed into the loan (or paid in cash at closing)
  • Must be either fully financed or fully paid in cash — no mixing
2️⃣ Annual Mortgage Insurance Premium (MIP)
  • Paid monthly with the mortgage payment
  • Based on LTV, loan term, and loan amount
  • Varies by borrower scenario
  • Except: Hawaiian Homelands Section 247 loans — no annual MIP

UFMIP Formula

UFMIP Calculation UFMIP = Base Loan Amount × 1.75%
Example:
Base Loan Amount: $300,000
UFMIP: $300,000 × 1.75% = $5,250
If financed, new loan amount: $300,000 + $5,250 = $305,250
UFMIP = $5,250 (financed or paid in cash)

Exceptions to the 1.75% UFMIP

  • Streamline refinances and simple refinances of FHA loans endorsed on or before May 31, 2009 — different amounts
  • Hawaiian Homelands — different UFMIP amounts, no monthly MIP
  • Indian Lands FHA program — no UFMIP required

Disclosure Rules — Critical for Processors

🚨 UFMIP must be disclosed on the Loan Estimate for every FHA loan.
  • Double-check it — sometimes the LO misses it
  • If UFMIP is missed, the Annual MIP cannot be calculated correctly
  • Wrong UFMIP on the LE = costly compliance cures
  • Verify the correct UFMIP is on the LE AND the annual MIP is reflected in the total proposed monthly payment

4. FHA Annual MI Chart Module 3 · Session 1

There are two annual MI charts — one for loans greater than 15 years and one for loans equal to or less than 15 years. Find the right chart based on the loan term.

Chart A — Loan Terms Greater Than 15 Years

≤ $726,200> 95%55 bps (0.55%)Mortgage term
> $726,200≤ 95%70 bps (0.70%)11 years

💡 Most common scenario: Loan amounts less than $726,200 with LTV greater than 95% (3.5% down payment). Annual MI = 0.55% for the duration of the mortgage term.

Chart B — Loan Terms Equal to or Less Than 15 Years

Base Loan AmountLTVAnnual MIP (bps)Duration
≤ $726,200≤ 90%15 bps (0.15%)11 years
≤ $726,200> 90%40 bps (0.40%)Mortgage term
> $726,200≤ 90%15 bps (0.15%)11 years
> $726,200> 90%40 bps (0.40%)Mortgage term
⚠️ Note the difference: For 15-year loans with 10%+ down payment (LTV ≤ 90%), the annual MIP drops to just 0.15% — much lower than the 30-year rate.

Annual MIP Calculation Example

Monthly MIP Calculation Monthly MIP = (Base Loan Amount × Annual MIP %) ÷ 12
Scenario: $300,000 base loan, 30-year, 3.5% down (LTV > 95%)
Annual MIP rate: 0.55% (from Chart A)
Annual MIP: $300,000 × 0.55% = $1,650/year
Monthly MIP: $1,650 ÷ 12 = $137.50/month
Monthly MIP = $137.50
🚨 Critical: If the UFMIP is disclosed incorrectly, the annual MIP won't be calculated right. Both must be correct on the LE.

5. FHA Program Types & ADP Codes Module 3 · Session 1

FHA categorizes eligible mortgage programs by their section of the National Housing Act. These sections are the basis for the FHA case number's last three digits.

Common FHA Program Sections

Section of the ActProgramDescription
203(b)Single FamilyStandard purchase and refinance for 1-4 unit properties — including detached condos
203(k)RehabilitationPurchase/refi of a home that needs repairs or improvements
203(b) with Repair EscrowHUD REOFor HUD-owned properties needing minor repairs

ADP Code — The 3-Digit Code After the FHA Case Number

The ADP code goes right after the FHA case number on all FHA-related forms:

  • FHA addendums
  • Initial 1003
  • Final 1003
  • Appraisal reports
  • Builder docs
🔑 Format: [10-digit FHA case number] – [3-digit ADP code]
Example: 123-4567890 – 703
Common ADP codes: 703 (purchase) · 734 (refinance)

Where the ADP Code Goes

1On the 1003 borrower information page — after the FHA case number
2On the 1003 lender loan information page — after the FHA case number, under Mortgage Type: FHA
3On all FHA addendums and disclosures
4On appraisal reports and builder documents

Proposed Housing Expense — Where MIP Goes on the 1003

On the 1003's Proposed Housing Expense section:

  • Mortgage Insurance line = monthly MIP amount
  • This confirms the monthly MIP has been disclosed
  • Line I = UFMIP (only if fully financed) — otherwise blank if paid in cash at closing
⚠️ UFMIP on Line I: If UFMIP is financed, enter the whole dollar amount on Line I. If this field is blank, that indicates the borrower is paying UFMIP in full at closing (as part of total cash to close).

6. HUD Addendum — Form 92900A Module 3 · Session 1

The HUD 92900A is the Addendum to the Uniform Residential Loan Application for FHA loans. It is now FHA-only — FHA and VA stopped sharing this form as of 2021/2023.

Timeline of the Current Form

  • Lenders could start using it: December 2020
  • Mandatory: FHA case numbers assigned on or after March 22, 2021

What the Form Captures

1Part 1: Identifying information — FHA case number, mortgage info, sponsor/agent info, borrower name/address, property address, TPO info if applicable
2Part 2: Borrower's consent to verify SSN — must be signed by each borrower, used to pull FHA case number assignment
3Part 3: Borrower notices/acknowledgement — borrowers must read and sign the bottom of page 2
4Page 3: DE Underwriter's approval method & appraisal acceptance — top paragraph for AUS-approved loans; bottom paragraph for manual underwrite; Section B completed by DE underwriter when appraisal is on file
5Page 4: Lender certification — signed by an authorized lender representative (or closer, insurer, or other authorized rep). No longer requires borrower signature.

New in This Version — Nice Features

  • Borrower name and FHA case number printed at the top of each page — helps identify pages if they get separated from the file
  • Fewer borrower signatures required
  • Clearer layout with consolidated sections

When to Complete

FHA requires:
  • Initial 92900A completed at application and dated by borrower — before the loan is underwritten
  • Final 92900A signed and dated at closing
💡 Mortgagee Letter 2020-49 in your handouts details all changes to the 92900A and lists which FHA disclosures were renewed. Review it to ensure you're using the current forms.

7. VA Form 26-1820 Module 3 · Session 1

The VA Form 26-1820 is the new VA addendum. As of 2023, VA no longer uses the combination of the 92900A (pages 1–2) and VA Form 26-1802a.

Replaces

  • Expired HUD 92900F
  • VA Form 26-1802a

What the Form Captures

  • Loan number and borrower information
  • Section 1: Purpose, amount, terms, and security of the loan
  • Section 2: Lender information (with signature and date)
  • Section 3: Veteran's certifications
  • Veteran's information and signature/date, plus any cosigner
🔑 Autopopulated from LOS: Most of this form pulls automatically from data entered into your Loan Origination System. Verify all applicable fields are complete and accurate.
⚠️ Timing for VA: VA does not specify exactly when this form must be signed. But it should be provided to the borrower(s) at application along with all required VA disclosures.

8. Eligible FHA Properties Module 3 · Session 1

FHA insures mortgages on real property secured by 1-4 units. The property must be:

Eligible Property Types

  • Detached or semi-detached dwellings
  • Manufactured housing
  • Townhouses or row houses
  • Individual units within FHA-approved condo projects
  • Properties in Planned Unit Developments (PUDs) — treated as SFR, assuming not also a condo or manufactured home

Property Location

Property must be located in:
  • The United States
  • Puerto Rico
  • Guam
  • U.S. Virgin Islands
  • Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands
  • American Samoa

1-4 Units — Occupancy Rule

⚠️ One unit must be borrower-occupied as their primary residence. The appraiser must complete the appraisal as a primary residence appraisal — not an income-based appraisal.

Appraisal Responsibility

The mortgagee is responsible for obtaining an appraisal to:

  • Verify the value of the property
  • Verify compliance with HUD's Minimum Property Standards (MPS)

Condos

FHA has an approved condo list. If the project isn't approved, some lenders can do a single-unit approval for the individual unit. Check both before moving forward.

9. Principal Residence Rules Module 3 · Session 1

A principal residence is a dwelling where the borrower maintains or will maintain their permanent place of residency, and typically occupies for the majority of the calendar year.

🚨 FHA Rule: A borrower may have only one principal residence at any time.

Secondary Residences — Not Permitted Without Approval

Secondary residences are NOT permitted for FHA unless written approval is obtained from the jurisdictional HOC (Homeownership Center) after they determine:

1Borrower has no other secondary residence
2The secondary residence won't be a vacation home or used primarily for recreational purposes
3Commuting distance to the borrower's workplace creates an undue hardship
4There is no affordable rental housing meeting the borrower's needs within 100 miles of the workplace
5Maximum mortgage amount is 85% of the lesser of appraised value or sales price

Required Documentation for Second Home

  • Verification of lack of affordable housing in the applicable area
  • Satisfactory explanation of the need for a secondary residence
  • Written evidence from a local real estate professional verifying lack of acceptable housing
💡 Example scenario: A borrower regularly travels and spends part of each month in a small town for work. The town has no reasonable rental properties — so purchasing a second home makes more sense than continuing to pay for hotels. This is a valid basis for a second home approval.

10. Multiple FHA Loan Exceptions Module 3 · Session 1

🚨 FHA Rule: A borrower can only have one FHA-insured loan at a time.

But there are four exceptions that allow a second FHA-insured mortgage:

Exception 1 — Relocation

Borrower may obtain another FHA-insured mortgage without selling the existing property if:
  • Relocating or relocated for an employment-related reason
  • Established new principal residence in an area more than 100 miles from the current principal residence
  • Relocation must be employer-mandated (not voluntary)
Documentation: Relocation agreement, offer letter if needed

Exception 2 — Increase in Family Size

Borrower may be eligible for another FHA-insured mortgage if:
  • Satisfactory evidence that borrower has had an increase in legal dependents
  • The current property now fails to meet family needs
  • LTV on current principal residence is equal to or less than 75%, or paid down to that amount
Documentation: Birth certificate (new baby), adoption papers, college transcripts (student returning home), appraisal showing ≥25% equity in current home

Exception 3 — Vacating a Jointly Owned Property

Borrower is vacating with no intent to return, and the property will remain occupied by an existing co-borrower:
  • Common in divorce situations — divorce decree awards home to ex-spouse who stays
  • Borrower vacating cannot afford large down payment or credit doesn't support conventional
  • Final signed divorce decree required (signed by all parties including the judge)
Documentation: Final divorce decree signed by judge

Exception 4 — Non-Occupying Co-Borrower

Non-occupying co-borrower on an existing FHA-insured mortgage may qualify for a new FHA-insured mortgage on their own primary residence:
  • Example: Parent signed a note/deed of trust for their son as a non-occupying co-borrower
  • Parent's credit shows FHA loan, but parent doesn't live there
  • Parent now wants to buy their own primary residence
Documentation: Copy of note, deed of trust, documents showing borrower was non-occupying co-borrower
💡 Processor tip: Always do a processor certification that clearly identifies which exception you're using. This helps anyone picking up the file understand the borrower's qualification path.

11. Property Flipping Rules Module 3 · Session 1

FHA has strict rules on property resales to prevent fraud and inflated values on flipped properties.

Seller Must Be Owner of Record

The title commitment and sales contract are used to determine the home is being sold by the legal owner.

Resale Time Restrictions

Resale PeriodFHA Eligibility
90 days or fewer from seller's acquisition date ❌ Not eligible for FHA-insured mortgage
91–180 days after acquisition ✅ Eligible with restrictions (see below)
More than 180 days ✅ Eligible with no restrictions

91–180 Day Resale Rules

⚠️ Second appraisal required if:
  • Resale occurs between 91–180 days after seller's acquisition
  • AND resale price is 100% or more over the price paid by the seller

Which Date to Use

Seller's Acquisition Date = Date on the warranty deed (not the HUD-1 / CD date)

Flipping Example

Scenario: Seller acquired property 95 days prior to new sales contract
Seller paid: $50,000
Seller now listing at: $125,000
Increase: ($125,000 − $50,000) ÷ $50,000 = 150%
150% > 100% threshold
Second appraisal required to support value

What If the Second Appraisal Comes in Lower?

If the second appraisal is more than 5% lower than the first appraisal:
  • The lower value must be used for determining property value
  • The cost of the second appraisal cannot be charged to the borrower — commonly paid by the seller or covered by the lender
⚠️ Important date check: Both the sales contract AND the FHA case number assignment must be dated after the 91st day of seller acquisition.

Exceptions to Time Restrictions

The following are exempt from the 90-day resale restriction:

  • Properties acquired by an employer or relocation agency in connection with employee relocation
  • Resales by HUD under its REO program
  • Sales by other U.S. government agencies of single-family properties pursuant to agency programs
  • Sales of properties by nonprofits approved to purchase HUD-owned properties at a discount with resale restrictions
  • Properties acquired by the seller by inheritance
  • Properties by state and federally chartered financial institutions and GSEs
  • Properties being sold by local and state government agencies
  • Sales of properties within PDMDAs (Presidentially Declared Major Disaster Areas) only with a notice of exception from HUD
  • Builders — new construction loans based on new purchase contracts from builders

13. Manual Underwriting Ratios Module 3 · Session 1

When the AUS returns a Refer, the loan must be manually underwritten. Manual underwriting has specific DTI and compensating factor requirements based on credit score.

Manual Downgrade Triggers

🚨 Loans must be manually downgraded if any of these apply:
  • Disputed derogatory accounts ≥ $1,000 (or multiple totaling $1,000+)
  • Loan does not meet FHA's significant derogatory event seasoning requirements
  • Late mortgage payment history
  • Undisclosed mortgage debt (e.g., seller financing not on credit report)
  • Self-employed income reflecting a decline of 20% or more over the most recent 2 years

Manual Underwrite Ratios by Credit Score

Credit ScoreMax RatiosCompensating Factors Required
Below 580 31/43 None (cannot exceed, even with comp factors)
Below 580 (Energy Efficient Mortgage) 33/45 Must use proceeds for energy efficient improvements
580+ 31/43 None
580+ 37/47 1 compensating factor required
580+ 40/40 No discretionary debt — mortgage must be the only open debt
580+ 40/50 2 compensating factors required

Compensating Factors

Cash Reserves

Verified with borrower's own funds:
• 3 months PITIA for 1-unit properties
• 6 months PITIA for 2-4 unit properties

Minimal Increase in Housing Payment

The housing payment is increasing by the lesser of:
• $100, or
• 5%

Residual Income

Calculated the same way as VA loans (detailed in VA section)

Significant Additional Income

Overtime, bonus, or second job income not reflected in effective income. Must be for a borrower on the loan — not a non-borrower spouse.

🚨 Absolute maximum: 50% back-end ratio for any manually approved FHA loan, even with compensating factors.

Manual vs. AUS Underwriting — Key Difference

An AUS approval (Accept Eligible) and a manual underwrite are completely different:
  • AUS approvals can go up to higher DTIs based on the algorithm
  • Manual underwriting has hard ratio caps by credit score
  • Manual requires documented compensating factors for higher ratios
Processors: Do due diligence before submitting to underwriting. If your file will trigger a manual downgrade, prepare the compensating factors first.

Session 1 Practice Quiz 10 Questions

1. All FHA borrowers pay two types of mortgage insurance premiums. What are they?

Upfront Mortgage Insurance Premium (UFMIP) and Annual MIP (paid monthly).

2. What section of the Act covers single-family homes?

Section 203(b)

3. True or False — Manufactured homes are not insured by FHA.

False. FHA does insure manufactured homes (up to maximum financing, subject to guidelines).

4. Name one exception to the FHA rule regarding number of mortgages per borrower.

Any of the four exceptions: Relocation, Increase in family size, Vacating a jointly owned property, or Non-occupying co-borrower.

5. A property resold within ___ days or fewer is not eligible for FHA financing.

90 days

6. Data integrity review includes what five things?

The credit report, debts, income, assets, and value of the property.

7. What certificate does a lender obtain to verify veteran eligibility?

Certificate of Eligibility (COE)

8. What is the basic VA entitlement dollar amount?

$36,000 (basic entitlement; bonus entitlement available for higher loan amounts).

9. True or False — The VA funding fee is 2.75% of the loan.

False. The VA funding fee depends on the down payment and the type of veteran. Unlike FHA's flat 1.75% UFMIP, VA funding fees vary.

10. A ___ veteran does not pay a VA funding fee.

Exempt — one who is exempt from the funding fee (shown on the COE). Non-exempt = pays. Some COEs show "Contact RLC" — meaning additional info needed.

1. What is VA? Module 3 · Session 2

VA stands for the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs. VA loans were created in 1944 through the Serviceman's Readjustment Act, commonly known as the GI Bill.

A VA loan is a mortgage guaranteed by the VA. The VA guarantees a portion of the loan against loss — helping lenders provide veterans with favorable financing terms.

🏛️ Agency

U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs (VA)

📅 Created

1944 by the GI Bill

🛡️ Purpose

Guarantee a portion of the loan against loss — helps veterans get favorable terms

🔑 Key concept: The VA does not lend money — it guarantees loans made by approved lenders. The guarantee protects the lender against loss if the veteran goes into foreclosure. But an eligible veteran still must meet credit and income standards to qualify.

Who VA Loans Are For

  • Eligible American veterans
  • Surviving spouses (with conditions — see Section 2)

2. Veteran Eligibility Module 3 · Session 2

Basic Eligibility Requirements

A veteran is eligible for VA home loan benefits if he or she:

  • Served on active duty in the Army, Navy, Air Force, Marine Corps, or Coast Guard after September 15, 1940
  • Was discharged under conditions other than dishonorable

Service Requirements (Post-WWII Veterans)

Service PeriodRequirement
Wartime 90 days or more, any part during wartime
Peacetime 181 continuous days or more

Post-1980 / Post-1981 Officers — 2-Year Requirement

For veterans who enlisted and service began after September 7, 1980, or who entered service as an officer after October 16, 1981, a greater length of service is required:
  • 24 continuous months of active duty, or
  • The full period for which called or ordered to active duty, but not less than 90 days (wartime) or 181 continuous days (peacetime)

Reserves & National Guard

Members of the Reserves or National Guard who are not otherwise eligible for loan guarantee benefits can qualify upon completion of 6 years of service — unless released earlier due to a service-connected disability.

⚠️ Discharge Status: The applicant must have received an honorable, general, or under honorable conditions discharge. "Under other than honorable conditions" is NOT qualifying, unless they are in active status awaiting final discharge, or still serving in the Reserves or Guard.

Surviving Spouses

Spouses of veterans may have loan purchase eligibility if they meet one of the following:

  • Unmarried surviving spouse of a veteran who died as a result of service or service-connected causes
  • Surviving spouse who remarried on or after age 57 and on or after December 16, 2003
  • Spouse of an active duty member listed as MIA or POW for at least 90 days — eligibility is one-time use only
💡 Note: Surviving spouses of veterans who died from non-service-connected causes may also be eligible under certain conditions. See VA guidelines for details — including the special codes on the COE.

3. Certificate of Eligibility (COE) Module 3 · Session 2

The Certificate of Eligibility (COE) is the only reliable proof of eligibility for a VA loan — except in the case of an Interest Rate Reduction Refinancing Loan (IRRRL).

Why the COE Matters

🚨 Critical rule: Lenders must establish eligibility BEFORE ordering an appraisal or closing a loan. VA cannot guarantee a loan for an ineligible veteran.

How to Obtain a COE

1Online (Preferred): Lenders use VA's WebLGY portal — login, go to Applications → Eligibility → follow prompts. Usually generates in seconds.
2Veteran self-service: Veteran can create an account on VA.gov and request it electronically
3By mail: Submit VA Form 26-1880 (Request for Certificate of Eligibility) to the Eligibility Center — slower but acceptable
🔑 Online is preferred — faster, and lets you know immediately if the veteran is eligible.

COE Information Requirements

  • Veteran's Social Security Number
  • Veteran's year of birth
  • For surviving spouses: use the veteran's SSN and DOB (not the spouse's)
  • Always request the DD214 or Statement of Service from the veteran — especially if the borrower is a surviving spouse

Certificate of Eligibility Codes — Contact RLC

⚠️ "Contact RLC" code: If the COE shows "Contact RLC" (Regional Loan Center), the system cannot generate a determination. You must mail in the request for a COE signed and dated by the veteran. This does NOT mean the veteran is ineligible — usually just needs additional information.
📌 Once the COE is received, generally no need to update it before loan closing, unless the lender has reason to believe it is inaccurate.

4. VA Entitlement Module 3 · Session 2

Entitlement is the amount available for use on a loan — the amount the VA is willing to guarantee.

Basic Entitlement $36,000
🔑 Basic entitlement is $36,000 — displayed near the center of the COE. It may be reduced if the veteran has used entitlement before and it has not been restored.

Bonus (Additional) Entitlement

For certain loans in excess of $144,000, additional bonus entitlement may be available.

Loan AmountMax Entitlement
Standard25% of the appropriate loan limit (varies by county)
Above $647,20025% of the county conforming loan limit
💡 The COE never shows bonus entitlement as a dollar figure. Instead, there will be an asterisk by the word "available" referring to a note explaining potential extra entitlement.

Blue Water Navy Vietnam Veterans Act (2019)

Effective January 1, 2020, this legislation provides 25% guarantee / automatic entitlement for VA loans with NO loan limits, provided:

  • The transaction is a purchase
  • The veteran has 100% entitlement
💡 Not just for Vietnam veterans. Despite the name, this legislation applies to all eligible veterans. However, to have full entitlement, the veteran:
  • Cannot have any other active VA loans
  • Any prior VA loans require the veteran to have sold the property (not just had entitlement reinstated for a refinance)
⚠️ If the veteran previously used entitlement that has not been restored, available entitlement is reduced by the amount used on the prior loan.

5. VA Funding Fee Module 3 · Session 2

The VA funding fee is very similar to the FHA upfront mortgage insurance premium (UFMIP). It is a one-time fee paid on most VA loans.

Funding Fee Status on the COE

✅ Exempt

Veteran does not pay the funding fee (e.g., certain disabled veterans).

❌ Non-Exempt

Veteran must pay the applicable funding fee.

📌 The funding fee status appears near the top of the COE. The exemption status is on the right. Always read the conditions field near the middle of the COE — it may have additional statements.

Three Types of Funding Fees

1Purchase & Construction Loans — for regular military, Reserves, and National Guard
2Cash-Out Refinances — higher fee tier
3IRRRL, Manufactured Home, and Loan Assumptions — lowest fee tier
⚠️ Funding fee is NOT a flat rate. It depends on:
  • Type of loan (purchase vs. refinance)
  • Down payment amount (for purchase loans)
  • Type of veteran (regular military vs. Reserves/Guard)
  • First use vs. subsequent use
🔑 Contrast with FHA: FHA has a flat 1.75% UFMIP across the board. VA funding fees vary. Never assume a single percentage.

How the Funding Fee Is Paid

  • Financed into the loan (most common), or
  • Fully paid in cash by the borrower at closing, or
  • Paid by the seller as an acceptable closing cost
🚨 Must be disclosed on the LE for every VA loan. Like UFMIP, the funding fee is a critical disclosure item. Double-check it.

6. VA Loan Purposes Module 3 · Session 2

VA loans can be used for:

Purchase & Construction

  • Purchase or construct a residence (including a condo unit) to be owned and occupied by the veteran as a home
  • Purchase a one-family residential unit in a VA-approved condo project
  • Purchase a farm residence to be owned and occupied by the veteran as a home

Refinance

  • Refinance an existing VA-guaranteed or direct loan to get a lower interest rate
  • Refinance an existing mortgage or other indebtedness secured by a lien of record on a residence owned and occupied by the veteran

Repair, Alter, or Improve

  • Repair, alter, or improve a residence owned by the veteran and occupied as a home
  • Simultaneously purchase and improve a home
  • Install solar heating/cooling systems, energy conservation measures, or additions
⚠️ Farm Residence: If the loan includes the purchase of farmland, the farmland is appraised at its residential value only. All farmland must be residential — not used for agricultural purposes.

7. VA Case Numbers & CAIVRS Module 3 · Session 2

VA Case Number (LIN)

The VA case number is a 12-digit VA loan identification number (LIN) assigned to each loan by VA at the time the appraisal is requested.

  • Used electronically in VA systems
  • Used on VA forms and other documents where requested
  • Ordered through WebLGY
🔑 Rule: You must obtain a COE before ordering an appraisal and LIN.

Ordering a VA Case Number

1Log into WebLGY
2Obtain COE first (required)
3Request the VA case number — usually ordered together with the appraisal
⚠️ VA appraisals take ~10 business days. Order with enough lead time. After the appraisal comes in, the underwriter reviews it and issues the Notice of Value (NOV).

IRRRL Case Numbers — Different Process

For Interest Rate Reduction Refinancing Loans (IRRRLs):

  • Credit qualification isn't always required
  • No appraisal is required
  • You are simply assigning a new VA case number — not ordering an appraisal
💡 Order IRRRL cases correctly based on the transaction type. Underwriters often handle these, but the processor may be asked to do it.

CAIVRS — Credit Alert Verification Reporting System

CAIVRS is a government system that reports individuals with outstanding federal debt.

Required For

Both FHA and VA loans

Where to Pull

Through the FHA Connection website

How to Pull

Click "CAIVRS" option → enter borrower's full name, SSN, and DOB

📌 For FHA: CAIVRS result is reflected on the initial FHA case number assignment. The CAIVRS number is attached to the FHA case number and inputted on the 1003 and LE.
📌 For VA: CAIVRS result must be included in the VA loan file.

8. VA Credit Analysis Module 3 · Session 2

Credit Report Requirements

  • A standard tri-merge credit report is required on all VA transactions
  • Must return credit scores and profiles from at least two bureaus
  • Public records information must be available

Alternative Credit Profile

If a borrower has no traditional credit:

  • The loan is downgraded to a manual underwrite
  • An alternative credit profile must be documented
  • Sources: rental history, car insurance, cell phone bills, utilities
  • Alternative credit cannot be used to overcome past poor credit — only for borrowers with little to no traditional credit history
⚠️ Two scores only? Go with the lower of the two. One score only? Check with your lender for acceptability.

Non-Purchasing Spouse (NPS) — Community Property States

Community property states require a credit report on the non-purchasing spouse.
  • Any debt listed on the NPS credit report must be included in liabilities and qualifying ratios
  • Borrower authorization must be assigned by the NPS and dated on or before the credit pull date

VA Credit Score Requirements

VA has no minimum credit score requirement. However, VA lenders generally look for a minimum 620 FICO — "Fair Credit."

Co-Signed Debts

Debts the borrower has co-signed must be included in the DTI unless the co-obligated party can provide:

  • Copies of canceled checks, bank statements, or other proof that they solely made payments for the last 12 months on time
Example: Borrower is co-signer on son's car note
Payment: $500/month
Without documentation of son's payments → include $500 in borrower's DTI
With 12 months of canceled checks or bank statements showing son paid → can exclude the payment
⚠️ Important: Review the actual note to verify both the son and the borrower are obligated. If only the borrower is obligated, the payment still counts — regardless of who pays.
Co-signed debt treatment hinges on documentation

Child Support & Alimony

  • Must be verified with a final signed and dated divorce decree (signed by the judge)
  • Use the child support or alimony amount reflected in the decree

VA Child Care Letter — Required on Every VA Loan

🚨 Executed VA Child Care letter must be in the file at initial submission — even if the veteran does not pay for child care.

VA Child Care Letter — Options

Option 1

Borrower pays for child care → include in DTI

Option 2

Borrower does not pay for child care

Option 3

Borrower has child care provided at no cost — provide explanation (e.g., grandparent or non-working spouse)

📌 Why it matters: Child care affects both DTI and residual income calculations.

Collections — VA Treatment

VA collections: Isolated collection accounts do not necessarily have to be paid off for loan approval. However:
  • Collection accounts must be considered as part of the overall credit history
  • Unpaid collections are considered open recent credit
  • Borrowers with collection history should have reestablished satisfactory credit — typically defined as no late payments for 12 months following the last derogatory event
💡 Processor tip: Ask for an explanation letter on collection accounts up front. It helps the underwriter understand the borrower's situation and often avoids additional conditions.

Student Loans — VA Treatment

VA differs from FHA on student loans:
  • If payments are scheduled to begin within 12 months of VA loan closing → include the anticipated monthly obligation
  • If borrower provides evidence the debt is deferred beyond 12 months → may exclude from analysis
  • Otherwise: use the credit report amount or documentation from the student loan servicer
  • If no payment is provided or verified: VA allows balance × 0.5% ÷ 12 months as the qualifying payment
VA Student Loan Payment (if none provided) Qualifying Payment = (Outstanding Balance × 0.5%) ÷ 12 months
⚠️ Compare to FHA: FHA uses the greater of 1% of balance or the documented payment. VA defaults to 0.5% of balance if nothing is provided. Different calculation — check which program you're on.

9. VA Income Analysis Module 3 · Session 2

Acceptable Income Sources

  • Wages from W-2 employment
  • Self-employment
  • Overtime, bonus, or commission
  • Part-time and seasonal employment (with 2-year history)
  • Military retirement and Social Security income
  • Alimony or child support
  • Notes receivable, interest, dividend income
  • Rental income (with 25% vacancy allowance if not on tax returns)
  • Car allowances (if documented 2 years on tax returns or pay stubs)
  • Trust income

Overtime, Bonus, or Commission

2-year history required.
  • Written VOE that breaks out overtime and continuance
  • Can come from different jobs — as long as the 24 consecutive months of receiving OT/bonus income is documented
  • Use 24-month average for qualifying

Social Security, VA, or Private Disability

  • Document borrower's receipt for the most recent 3 months (bank statements preferred)
  • Obtain documentation establishing the award benefits — typically an award letter
  • If the award doesn't have a defined expiration date → may consider the income effective and reasonably likely to continue
🚨 HIPAA-protected: Never inquire into or request documentation about the nature of a disability or medical condition. This is prohibited by HIPAA laws.

Active Military Income

  • Original or certified copy of most recent LES (Leave and Earnings Statement)
  • 2 years most recent W-2s
  • If release from active duty occurs within 12 months of anticipated closing date:
    • Verification of a valid offer of local civilian employment, OR
    • Evidence of reenlistment + statement of intent to reenlist, AND
    • Statement from commanding officer that borrower is eligible to reenlist

Military Allowances — BAS & BAH

Active military borrowers may have housing and clothing allowances (BAH and BAS) which are not taxed and can be grossed up by 25%.

Military Allowance Gross-Up Grossed-Up Amount = Non-Taxable Allowance × 1.25

Understanding the LES

The Leave and Earnings Statement (LES) is given to active-duty military. It shows:

  • Gross pay and taxable wages
  • Breakdown of regular pay, overtime, allotments, and deductions
  • Any garnishments or child support payments (in the deductions section)
  • Leave benefits paid by the government
⚠️ Pay attention to deductions on the LES for garnishments or child support payments. Allotments should be researched to determine if they're debt repayments that should be included in qualification.

Two-Year Employment History

Required on all VA loans.
  • Generally, employment less than 12 months is NOT considered stable
  • Exception: recent college graduates with official school transcripts and a copy of their degree
  • Income must be stable, reliable, documented, and likely to continue for a minimum of 3 years after closing

Workers Compensation

Documentation requirements:

  • Award letter
  • Most recent pay stubs covering one full month
  • Documentation of continuance

Social Security and Pension

  • Award letter, AND/OR
  • 60 days of bank statements showing receipt
  • Documentation of 3-year continuance if NOT a lifetime benefit

10. VA Asset Analysis Module 3 · Session 2

Acceptable Asset Sources

  • Borrower's checking or savings accounts
  • Retirement account funds
  • Liquidation of stocks
  • Gifts from allowable sources
  • Proceeds from the sale of a prior residence

Documentation Requirements

  • Checking and savings: Prior month's balance + all pages of statements
  • Retirement accounts: Every page of statements
  • Liquidation of stocks: Document that it has been liquidated + paper trail
  • Gifts: Gift letter from allowable source + evidence of transfer
  • Proceeds from sale of prior residence: Final CD or settlement statement

Large Deposits

⚠️ All statements must be reviewed for large or irregular deposits. Large deposits should be:
  • Identified
  • Documented
  • Sourced

Also review statements for any payments to creditors that do not appear on the credit report — these may identify undisclosed debt.

Cash to Close

  • Borrower provides most current bank statement with previous balance to verify cash to close
  • If the statement doesn't reflect previous month's balance → provide two full months of bank statements

Earnest Money Deposit

VA requires:
  • Verify and document the deposit amount
  • Source of funds if the amount exceeds 1% of the sales price
  • OR if it's excessive based on the borrower's history of accumulating savings

Gift Letter Requirements — VA

Required contents:
  • Applicant name and loan number
  • Donor's name and relationship
  • Gift amount
  • Recipient (borrower)
  • Property address
  • Source of donor's funds
  • Signature + printed name + address + donor phone number
  • Signature of all borrowers on the loan
🚨 Cousins are NOT considered family members for gift funds on VA loans. If a cousin is the donor, they need to find another donor.

Transfer of Gift Funds — Three Scenarios

1Already in borrower's account: Donor's bank statement showing withdrawal + evidence of deposit into borrower's account
2Not yet in borrower's account: Certified check, money order, cashier's check, or wire transfer evidencing payment + donor's bank statement showing sufficient funds
3Gift to settlement agent directly: Gift letter + cashier's check/money order payable to settlement agent + donor's bank statement showing the funds cleared
⚠️ Pending is not enough. The withdrawal must be cleared from the donor's account, and the deposit must be cleared in the borrower's account (or with the settlement agent). No pending transactions.

Interested Party Contributions (IPCs) — VA

🚨 VA max seller concessions = 4% of the purchase price.

What Counts as a VA Seller Concession

Anything of value added to the transaction by the builder or seller for which the buyer pays nothing additional and the seller is not customarily expected to pay:

  • Payment of the buyer's VA funding fee
  • Prepayment of buyer's property taxes and insurance
  • Gifts such as a television or microwave oven
  • Payment of extra points to provide permanent interest rate buy-downs
  • Provision of escrow funds to provide temporary interest rate buy-downs
  • Payoff of credit balances or judgments on behalf of the buyer

What Does NOT Count as a VA Seller Concession

  • Payment of the buyer's closing costs
  • Payment of points as appropriate to the market

Reserves — VA

VA requires:
  • Verify and document all assets submitted to the AUS Total Scorecard
  • Verify reserves equivalent to 3 months PITI after closing for 3-to-4-unit properties

11. Residual Income Module 3 · Session 2

Residual income is a VA-specific calculation that ensures the veteran has enough money left over each month after paying housing and all other debts. It's a key qualifying factor on VA loans.

Residual Income Formula Residual Income = Total Qualifying Income − PITIA − All Other Monthly Debts
🔑 Residual income must meet a minimum threshold based on:
  • Loan amount
  • Family size (including non-borrowing spouse and dependents)
  • Region of the country

Why Residual Income Matters

  • VA loans have no maximum DTI (unlike conventional or FHA)
  • Instead, VA requires residual income to meet minimums
  • This is how VA ensures veterans can afford the loan
💡 AUS will calculate residual income automatically on VA loans. But understanding the concept helps when working with underwriters or explaining it to borrowers.

Session 2 Practice Quiz 10 Questions

1. What year was the VA loan created?

1944 — through the Serviceman's Readjustment Act (GI Bill).

2. What does the VA do for a loan — lend or guarantee?

Guarantees. The VA guarantees a portion of the loan against loss — it does not lend money.

3. What document is the only reliable proof of eligibility for a VA loan?

Certificate of Eligibility (COE)

4. What is the basic VA entitlement amount?

$36,000 — displayed near the center of the COE.

5. True or False — The VA funding fee is a flat 1.75% of the loan.

False. That's the FHA UFMIP. VA funding fees vary based on loan type, down payment, veteran type, and first use vs. subsequent use.

6. What does CAIVRS stand for, and where is it pulled?

Credit Alert Verification Reporting System. Pulled through the FHA Connection website. Required for both FHA and VA loans.

7. VA student loans — what's the default calculation if no payment is provided?

Balance × 0.5% ÷ 12 months. (Different from FHA's 1% rule.)

8. True or False — Cousins are considered family members for VA gift funds.

False. Cousins are NOT considered family members for gift funds.

9. What is the maximum VA seller concession?

4% of the purchase price. (Compare FHA = 6%.)

10. What is residual income?

Total qualifying income − PITIA − All other monthly debts. VA-specific calculation. Must meet a minimum based on loan amount, family size, and region.

3. FHA Appraisal Logging Module 3 · Session 3

Appraisal logging is the process of recording the appraisal information in FHA Connection. Some lenders require the processor to do this; others have the underwriter handle it.

When Appraisal Logging Is Used

  • Non-endorsed cases assigned an FHA case number prior to 06/27/2017 → use FHA Connection appraisal logging
  • Cases assigned on or after 06/27/2017 → use FHA's Electronic Appraisal Delivery (EAD) portal to upload the appraisal

Why Appraisal Logging Matters

  • Logging the appraisal triggers FHA's property flipping rules check
  • If a second appraisal is required, the system will tell you
  • Inputs prior sale / transfer dates — critical for flips
🚨 Critical: Inputting the wrong date in the appraisal logging could cause FHA to not insure the loan. Always verify prior sale / transfer dates from the HUD-1 or CD.

Key Fields on Appraisal Logging

  • FHA case number
  • Property information (construction code, year built, effective age)
  • PUD information
  • Appraisal received date
  • Location, land use, predominant neighborhood price
  • Site area, square footage
  • Manufactured housing indicator
  • Foundation, total rooms, bedrooms, baths, living area
  • Central AC, garage type
  • Fee simple / leasehold indicator
  • Prior sale / transfer information (within past 3 years)
  • Date of prior sale / transfer (acquisition date)
  • Prior sale / transfer as result of family gift or inheritance (yes/no)
  • Appraisal type
  • Appraised value
  • Appraiser's ID
⚠️ Don't trust the EAD to auto-populate everything. Always double-check the fields after the AMC uploads the appraisal. Missing data = wrong logging = potential denial.

4. VA Notice of Value (NOV) Module 3 · Session 3

The Notice of Value (NOV) is the VA's official acceptance of the appraisal. It's issued by a VA-approved SAR underwriter after reviewing the fee appraiser's report.

VA Appraisal System — TOSS → WebLGY

The VA appraisal system is called TOSS — including requesting appraisals and issuing NOVs. As of 2023, these functions have been moved to WebLGY.

NOV Timing

⏱️ The NOV must be issued within 5 days of receipt of appraisal. The NOV is reviewed by an underwriter certified by VA to issue it.

NOV Delivery to Borrower

  • Copy of NOV provided to borrower with a copy of the appraisal
  • Do NOT provide the appraisal to the borrower until the NOV has been issued
  • Follow your company's policy on appraisal delivery procedures

NOV Contents

  • Date of the NOV
  • Lender loan number
  • VA case number
  • Appraisal reviewer (SAR)
  • Property address
  • Purchaser's name and mailing address
  • Estimated reasonable value determination
  • Any required repairs or conditions
  • Any lien-supported assessments, HOA fees, condo requirements
  • Lead water distribution system (if applicable)
  • Other conditions or requirements
  • Signature by VA Lab SAR

VA Appraisal Expiration

🚨 VA appraisals are good for up to 6 months. The expiration date is listed on the NOV. As the processor, you're responsible for tracking this and making sure it doesn't expire.

Property Conditions That Trigger Additional Inspection

If the appraiser cannot determine that a property meets FHA's MPR / MPS guidelines, an inspection by a qualified individual or entity may be required. Conditions that trigger this include:

  • Standing water against foundation and/or excessively damp basement
  • Hazardous materials on site or within improvements
  • Faulty or defective mechanical systems (electrical, plumbing, HVAC)
  • Evidence of possible structural failure (settlement, bulging foundation walls, unsupported floor joists, cracked masonry)
  • Evidence of possible pest infestation
  • Leaking or worn-out roofs
  • Any other condition that in the professional judgment of the appraiser warrants inspection

7. TRID Overview & Application Module 3 · Session 3

TRID stands for TILA-RESPA Integrated Disclosure Rule. It was designed to help borrowers understand the terms of their home financing transaction.

💡 Also known as the "Know Before You Owe" rule — TRID is often referred to by this nickname.

Timeline

  • Rolled out in October 2015
  • Before TRID: fees were on the HUD-1 Settlement Statement
  • After TRID: fees are on the Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure

What Constitutes an Application (TRID)

Six items submitted together:

1Consumer's name
2Consumer's income
3Consumer's Social Security Number (to obtain credit report)
4Property address
5Estimate of the value of the property
6The loan amount sought
📌 Once these 6 items are received, an application is triggered. The 3-day clock for the LE begins. If you can't make a loan for that borrower, a denial letter must be provided.

Business Days — Two Definitions

Specific Business Day

All days the lender is substantially open for business — generally excludes Saturdays, Sundays, and holidays.

Used for LE delivery.

General Business Day

All calendar days except Sundays and federal holidays.

Used for CD delivery, mailbox rule, and waiting periods.

Mailbox Rule

📬 Presumption: The borrower is presumed to receive a disclosure 3 general business days after it is delivered or placed in the mail.

Intent to Proceed

  • Borrower's consent to move forward with the transaction
  • Recommended format: signed acknowledgment form (wet or e-signature)
  • May also be expressed verbally, in person, by phone, or by email
🚨 No fees may be imposed before the borrower has received the LE and indicated intent to proceed. Exception: the credit report fee is allowed.

8. Loan Estimate (LE) Module 3 · Session 3

What the LE Replaced

  • Good Faith Estimate (GFE)
  • Initial Truth in Lending (TIL) disclosure

When It's Required

LE Delivery Deadline No later than 3 specific business days after application
And No Later Than 7 general business days prior to closing

Exceptions

  • If application is denied or withdrawn within the first 3 business days → LE not required
  • Once the application is denied/withdrawn, it cannot be reactivated
  • If the borrower wants to proceed later, they must reapply for a new loan

Accompanying Documents

  • Your Home Loan Toolkit (CFPB) — required on first-lien purchases
  • Written List of Service Providers — if the borrower can shop for a service
⚠️ On refinances, an alternative LE format may be provided (different tables for costs at closing).

Delivery Timeline Example

Scenario: New application received Monday the 2nd
Initial LE must be sent by Thursday the 5th
If mailed, considered received Monday the 9th
If quick closing is required, loan could not close before Friday the 13th (7 days after LE mailing)
Additionally, CD must be received Tuesday the 10th (3 business days prior to closing)
Sequence: Application → LE → 7-day wait → CD → 3-day wait → Closing

9. Closing Disclosure (CD) Module 3 · Session 3

What the CD Replaced

  • HUD-1 Settlement Statement
  • Final Truth in Lending (TIL) disclosure

Delivery Requirement

CD Delivery Deadline Received by borrower at least 3 general business days before consummation

Key Rules

  • Must contain the actual terms and costs of the transaction
  • Uses the same format and fee names as the LE
  • Cannot be delivered the same day as an LE is issued
  • Must be given separately to each consumer holding rescission rights
  • All consumers with an ownership interest who occupy as primary residence must receive their own copy — even if not a borrower on the loan

Non-Borrowing Spouse Example

Scenario: Borrower's spouse will not sign the Note but is entitled to rescind the transaction.
  • The spouse must receive a separate copy of the CD 3 business days prior to closing
  • The broker is responsible for obtaining delivery information for the non-borrowing party entitled to rescission rights

CD Revisions — Before Consummation

Requires New 3-Day Waiting Period
  • APR becomes inaccurate
  • Loan product changes (e.g., fixed to ARM)
  • Prepayment penalty added
Does NOT Require New Wait
  • Increase in hazard insurance paid by borrower
  • Walkthrough or last-minute adjustments made by seller or buyer
  • Small title changes

CD Corrections After Consummation

  • Corrected CD required if an event causes the CD to become inaccurate
  • Must be within 30 days after consummation
  • Used to document refunds for tolerance violations or correct clerical errors

CD Accuracy Rules

  • As accurate as possible
  • Interest rate must be locked and reflected on the LE before CD
  • Revised CDs may be issued under change of circumstance — but cannot be used to reset a tolerance
  • Any changes must stay within the applicable tolerance
  • A revised CD may extend an existing waiting period OR incur a new 3-day wait if APR increases more than 1/8 of 1%

10. Tolerance Buckets Module 3 · Session 3

TRID has three tolerance buckets that limit how much fees can increase from LE to CD.

🔴 0% Tolerance

Cannot increase at all — even with change of circumstance.

  • Lender charges and credits
  • Mortgage broker charges
  • Fees paid to affiliate of creditor or broker
  • Unaffiliated third-party if shopping is not allowed
  • Transfer taxes
🟡 10% Cumulative

Total cannot increase more than 10%.

  • Recording fees
  • Unaffiliated third-party if shopping is allowed
  • Services for which the consumer may shop but did not
🟢 No Tolerance / Good Faith

Can increase freely if good faith.

  • Prepaid interest
  • Amounts placed in escrow
  • Property insurance premiums
  • Charges paid to third-party not on written list
  • Charges paid to third-party not required by creditor

Services the Consumer CANNOT Shop For

These are in the 0% tolerance bucket:
  • Credit report fee
  • Appraisal fee
  • FHA UFMIP
  • VA funding fee

Services the Consumer CAN Shop For

In the 10% cumulative bucket (if they choose from the lender's list):

  • Title insurance
  • Settlement agent
🚨 0% tolerance fees never move. If they do, the lender must cure — usually with a refund.

11. Change of Circumstance Module 3 · Session 3

A change of circumstance is a valid reason to revise the LE. Without one, fees cannot change beyond tolerance.

Valid Changes of Circumstance

  • Interest rate locked or rate lock extended
  • Consumer requests a change to loan terms that increases settlement charges
  • Settlement costs increase beyond tolerance due to a valid changed circumstance
  • Appraisal fee increase because property turns out to be multi-unit (not single family)
  • Unreleased lien discovered → additional title fee
  • War or natural disaster
  • Title insurer goes out of business
  • Boundary dispute or pest inspection reveals termite damage
  • Underwriter determines less income, one borrower becomes unemployed, or loan program change requires appraisal

Revised LE Timing

Revised LE Delivery Within 3 business days of the changed circumstance
📌 Also: The applicant must receive the revised LE no later than 4 business days prior to the closing date.
⚠️ Once the CD has been delivered, a revised LE cannot be issued.

Lender Bound By LE

🚨 Creditors are bound by the LE. Revisions are only permitted in limited changed circumstances that occur after the LE is provided.

Example — UFMIP and VA Funding Fee

💡 Correct the UFMIP and VA funding fee disclosure immediately. If they're wrong on the LE, you may not be able to change them without a valid change of circumstance. This is one of the most common compliance issues.

Session 3 Practice Quiz 10 Questions

1. An appraiser requires the ___ at appraisal order (for both FHA and VA).

Sales contract — with all addendums, and reflecting seller contributions.

2. New construction loans require ___.

Builder documents — HUD 92541, HUD 92544, and termite docs (NPMA-99-A and 99-B if applicable).

3. True or False — Condos do not need to be approved through FHA or VA.

False. All condos for FHA and VA must be on the FHA Approved Condo List or VA Approved Condo List.

4. The Loan Estimate replaced the ___ and ___ disclosures.

Good Faith Estimate and the initial Truth in Lending (TIL).

5. The Closing Disclosure replaced the ___ and the ___.

HUD-1 and the final Truth in Lending (TIL).

6. Name one 0% tolerance fee.

Lender charges and credits (among others — mortgage broker charges, transfer taxes, services not shoppable).

7. What is a general business day?

All days except Sundays and federal holidays. (Compare to specific business day = days lender is substantially open.)

8. What disclosure is the lender bound by?

The Loan Estimate (LE).

9. A consumer must receive the CD at least ___ days before closing.

3 general business days.

10. True or False — A CD may be delivered at the same time as an LE.

False. A CD can never be delivered on the same day as an LE.
